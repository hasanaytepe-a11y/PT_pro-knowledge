PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Forward Head Posture

Definition and mechanism of development

Forward Head Posture is a postural disorder in which the head is positioned forward relative to the vertical axis.

It usually develops as a result of prolonged computer or phone use, poor sitting habits, and weak neck and back muscles.

This condition is accompanied by increased cervical lordosis, thoracic kyphosis, and forward rounding of the shoulders.

Exercise approach
  • Chin tuck exercise: Strengthens the deep cervical flexors.
  • Scapular retraction: Activates the middle–lower trapezius and rhomboid muscles.
  • Thoracic extension exercises: Can be performed on a foam roller or bolster.
  • Pectoral stretch: Stretch the chest muscles in a doorway or against a wall.
  • Postural awareness: Develop an upright sitting habit by maintaining head–shoulder–pelvis alignment.
Points to consider
  • Using a screen for prolonged periods with the head bent forward
  • Sitting hunched with the shoulders slumped forward
  • Carrying a heavy backpack
  • Performing forceful, sudden neck extension/rotation movements
  • Sleeping without a pillow or with a very high pillow
