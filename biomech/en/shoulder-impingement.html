PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Shoulder Impingement Syndrome

Definition and mechanism of development

Shoulder impingement syndrome is a functional disorder caused by compression of the rotator cuff tendons, subacromial bursa, and long head of the biceps tendon within the narrow space beneath the acromion.

Impaired scapular control, rotator cuff weakness, muscle imbalances around the shoulder, and repetitive overhead activities are risk factors. The supraspinatus tendon and subacromial bursa are affected most often.

Exercise approach
  • Scapular control: Scapular retraction–depression training with low-angle rowing-type movements
  • Rotator cuff strengthening: External rotation, “T–Y” stabilization exercises, prone arm raise (thumb up)
  • Serratus anterior and lower/middle trapezius activation: Wall slides and scapular control with a resistance band
  • Chest and posterior capsule stretching: Sleeper stretch, cross-body stretch
  • Functional progression: Gradual return to sport-/occupation-specific overhead activities
Points to consider
  • Overhead activities, particularly positions with the arm elevated between 70–120°
  • Loading that raises the arm forward and brings it into internal rotation
  • Hanging, pulling, or movements that create traction at the shoulder
  • Uncontrolled shoulder movements performed without scapular stabilization
  • Resisted exercises that cause pain or involve raising the arm above 90°
