PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Achilles Tendinopathy

Definition and mechanism of development

Achilles tendinopathy is a tendon disorder that most often develops due to excessive or unaccustomed loading and is degenerative rather than inflammatory in nature.

Increased loading (volume, speed, incline), changes in surface or footwear, and insufficient recovery time are the most common triggers.

Biomechanical factors such as calf muscle weakness, foot pronation, or limited dorsiflexion increase the risk.

Exercise approach
  • Eccentric loading (Alfredson protocol): Heel-drop exercises with straight- and bent-knee variations, twice daily for 12 weeks
  • Modified eccentric loading (in insertional cases): Simple ground-level loading without lowering the heel below floor level
  • Gastrocnemius–soleus stretching and posterior-chain myofascial mobilization
  • Load management: Gradual progression, continuing with tolerable pain, and long-term maintenance exercises (6–12 months)
  • Insole or heel support: When pronounced pronation or a sensation of compression is present
Points to consider
  • Prolonged complete rest or inactivity (reduces load-bearing capacity)
  • Sudden increases in load, rapid activities, or activities involving changes of direction
  • Deep heel-drop eccentric loading or aggressive stretching in insertional cases (increases compression)
  • Massage or intensive manual therapy in the presence of peritendinitis
  • Surgical approaches (conservative exercise treatment should be prioritized)
