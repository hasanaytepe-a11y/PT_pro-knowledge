Cervical Disc Herniation

Definition and mechanism of development

Cervical disc herniation occurs when the gel-like material within a disc between the cervical vertebrae protrudes through the outer ring and presses on the nerve roots or spinal cord. It generally develops as a result of age-related disc degeneration or sudden trauma.

The deep cervical flexors, middle–lower trapezius, and rhomboids are weakened; the upper trapezius, levator scapulae, scalene, and pectoral muscles are shortened and tight.

Exercise approach
  • Strengthening the deep neck flexors with the chin tuck exercise
  • Scapular retraction and stabilization exercises (for the middle–lower trapezius and rhomboids)
  • Standing row exercise (with a TheraBand)
  • Pectoral and upper trapezius stretches
  • Gentle, pain-free mobilizations: controlled rotation and lateral flexion movements
Points to consider
  • Overhead movements such as the shoulder press, military press, and dumbbell shoulder press should be avoided
  • Heavy lifting, prolonged driving, and repetitive overhead activities should be avoided
  • Prolonged forward bending or remaining with the neck rotated
  • Spending prolonged periods at a computer and poor sleeping positions
  • Neck extension, protraction, or bending toward the affected side
