PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Meniscal Tear

Definition and mechanism of development

The meniscus is a fibrocartilaginous structure that distributes load between the femur and tibia and contributes to joint stability.

Tears generally occur as a result of sudden movements combining rotation and loading while the knee is bent. The medial meniscus tears more often because it is less mobile. Age-related degenerative tears develop through repetitive microtrauma.

Exercise approach
  • Strengthening the quadriceps (particularly the vastus medialis oblique) and hamstrings
  • Activation of the hip abductor and extensor muscles
  • Closed-chain exercises: Mini squat, step-up, bridge, single-leg balance
  • Balance and proprioception exercises
  • Low-impact cardio: Light cycling, walking, swimming
Points to consider
  • Deep squatting, sudden pivoting, and rotational movements
  • Continuing exercise with locking “bucket handle” tears
  • Rapid return to sport or aggressive loading at an early stage
  • Exercising in a painful range or continuing to load as swelling increases
  • Prolonged immobilization and inactivity
