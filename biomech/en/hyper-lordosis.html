Hyperlordosis (Increased Lumbar Curve)

Definition and mechanism of development

Hyperlordosis is a postural disorder characterized by the lumbar lordosis angle exceeding normal limits (generally >40°–45°). The primary cause is muscle imbalance and forward tilting of the pelvis (anterior tilt).

Short/tight muscles: Psoas–iliacus, hamstrings, some paravertebral muscles
Weak/lengthened muscles: Abdominal muscles, gluteal group, deep pelvic stabilizers

Over time, muscle imbalance and poor core control may increase the lumbar curve, impair movement quality, and raise the risk of low back pain and lower-extremity injury.

Exercise approach

The most effective approach for hyperlordosis is a combination of stretching and strengthening. Stretching alone or strengthening alone has not been found sufficient.

  • Psoas–iliacus stretching
  • Hamstring stretching
  • Deep core muscles (TA, IO) – abdominal strengthening
  • Gluteal activation
  • Pelvic control exercises

Program duration is generally 8–12 weeks, and combination programs performed in 2–3 sessions per week have shown the best results. Some studies have reported that this combined approach is as effective as manual therapy.

Points to consider
  • One-dimensional interventions based only on stretching or only on strengthening may be insufficient.
  • The lordosis angle may respond more modestly to exercise than other postural disorders such as thoracic kyphosis.
  • The same measurement method should be used during follow-up (flexicurve, inclinometer, spinal mouse).
  • If core and pelvic stability are poor, neuromuscular control is impaired and the risk of low back pain and lower-extremity injury increases.
