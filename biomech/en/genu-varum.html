PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Bow Legs (Genu Varum)

Definition and mechanism of development

Genu varum is a knee alignment disorder characterized by an angle of less than 180 degrees between the axes of the femur and tibia, in which the knees remain apart.

Postural genu varum develops as a result of femoral internal rotation, knee hyperextension, and foot pronation; in the structural type, bony curvatures and developmental anomalies play a role.

In the long term, excessive loading on the inner side of the knee increases pain, loss of function, and the risk of osteoarthritis.

Exercise approach
  • Combined strengthening and neuromuscular exercises: Strengthening the quadriceps, hip, and pelvic stabilizers (3 days per week for 12 weeks)
  • Stretching exercises: Regular stretching of the hamstring and adductor muscles
  • Elastic-band exercises: Training the hip abductors and extensors
  • Gluteus medius–focused exercises: Pelvic drop and wall press (with hip internal rotation)
  • Balance and neuromuscular control: Single-leg balance, controlled landing, and foot-arch activation
Points to consider
  • Expecting exercise alone to correct bony deformities
  • Excessive internal rotation or aggressive stretching movements
  • High-impact activities (jumping, running on hard surfaces)
  • Wall press exercises performed in external rotation (may increase TFL activity)
  • Weight-bearing on one leg or uncontrolled landing positions
