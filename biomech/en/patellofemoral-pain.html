Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome

Definition and mechanism of development

Patellofemoral pain syndrome is a condition characterized particularly by anterior knee pain felt around or behind the patella.

It occurs most often in runners, young adults, and active individuals. Repetitive loading of the patellofemoral joint is associated with quadriceps and hip muscle weakness, iliotibial band (ITB) tightness, and biomechanical alignment disorders (e.g., knee valgus, pes planus).

Exercise approach
  • Quadriceps strengthening: Closed-chain exercises such as lunges, wall squats, and leg presses (at low flexion angles)
  • Hip abductor and external rotator strengthening: Step-up, lateral walking (with a resistance band)
  • ITB, hamstring, and gastrocnemius–soleus stretching: Foam rolling or dynamic stretching
  • Patellar taping or use of insoles: To increase load tolerance and reduce pain
  • Walking and running mechanics training: Knee alignment and femoral rotation control
Points to consider
  • Deep squatting, stair running, or activities involving high knee flexion
  • Forcing painful exercises or rapidly increasing training load
  • Relying only on passive treatments (massage, heat application)
