PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Knock Knees (Genu Valgum)

Definition and mechanism of development

Genu valgum is a knee alignment disorder characterized by a medial angle greater than 195° between the femur and tibia, in which the knees move toward each other while the ankles remain apart.

Femoral internal rotation, hip and knee muscle imbalances, pes planus, lateral muscle tightness, and medial muscle weakness may lead to this disorder.

Over time, load transfer shifts to the lateral compartment, stress on the patellofemoral joint increases, and the risk of osteoarthritis rises.

Exercise approach
  • Stretching exercises: Gentle stretches for the iliotibial band (ITB) and biceps femoris
  • Adductor strengthening: Pillow-squeeze exercises between the knees
  • Hip abductor and extensor exercises: Bridge, side-lying leg raise, mini squat
  • Elastic-band exercises: Gluteus medius, vastus lateralis, activation of the tibialis anterior and semitendinosus (3 days per week for 14 weeks)
  • Postural awareness: Keeping the knees in a neutral position and using appropriate orthotic support
Points to consider
  • Prolonged weight-bearing on one leg or sitting with the knees pressed together
  • Excessive internal rotation or high-impact activities (jumping, running on hard surfaces)
  • Applying direct pressure to the knee joint with a foam roller
  • Attempting to correct a bony deformity through exercise alone
  • Viewing taping or knee braces as a permanent solution
