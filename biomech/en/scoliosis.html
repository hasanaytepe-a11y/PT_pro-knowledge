PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Scoliosis

Definition and mechanism of development

Scoliosis is a three-dimensional trunk deformity resulting from lateral curvature of the spine together with rotation around its own axis.

Most cases are idiopathic, and progression may occur—particularly during growth—depending on age, sex, and the angle of curvature. Pulmonary function may decrease with thoracic or thoracolumbar curves; cardiorespiratory capacity is also affected in advanced cases.

Exercise approach
  • Breathing control and trunk awareness: Active Schroth exercises and rotational breathing directed toward the curve
  • Pelvic and scapular alignment: Postural correction using mirror or elastic-band feedback
  • Symmetrical core activation: Plank variations and exercises focused on a neutral spine
  • Balance and proprioception: Single-leg balance and balance-board exercises
  • Mobilization and elongation: Controlled, pain-free, and as symmetrical as possible movements
Points to consider
  • Fast, uncontrolled trunk rotations or turns using momentum
  • Exercises involving excessive extension (backward bending)
  • Heavy squats or overhead presses that place high axial loads on the spine
  • Positions that create static and intense neural tension
  • Excessively unilateral or uncontrolled “auto-elongation” attempts
