PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Spondylolisthesis

Definition and mechanism of development

Spondylolisthesis is a spinal stability disorder in which one vertebra slips forward over the vertebra below it.

The most common form is slippage of L5 over S1. It may be associated with pars interarticularis stress fractures, facet joint structure, disc degeneration, and loading during growth.

The deep core muscles (particularly the transversus abdominis, internal oblique, and multifidus) generally show delayed or insufficient activation; this weakens control of anterior spinal slippage.

Exercise approach
  • Core stabilization: Drawing-in maneuver, deep abdominal activation, controlled breathing
  • Bird-dog: Opposite arm–leg extension while maintaining a neutral spine
  • Plank variations: Side and front plank — low load, high control
  • Dead bug and supine leg raise: Controlled movements performed without disrupting the neutral spine position
  • Hamstring stretching: To reduce pelvic tilt and lumbar load
Points to consider
  • Prolonged sitting and forward bending involving lumbar flexion
  • Heavy lifting and sudden pulling–pushing movements
  • Fast and repetitive lumbar rotations
  • Excessive hyperextension and the Valsalva maneuver
  • Uncontrolled, aggressive exercises involving high loads
