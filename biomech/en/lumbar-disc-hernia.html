PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Lumbar Disc Herniation

Definition and mechanism of development

Lumbar disc herniation is a condition involving pain, numbness, or weakness in the lower back and legs when a disc between the vertebrae protrudes outward and presses on the nerve roots.

Prolonged sitting, poor posture, insufficient physical activity, and weakened lower-back muscles place excessive load on the discs. It occurs most often at the L4–L5 and L5–S1 levels; the multifidus and transversus abdominis muscles weaken, reducing spinal stability.

Exercise approach
  • Controlled activation of the deep core muscles: Exercises focusing on the transversus abdominis and multifidus
  • Strengthening the hip and gluteal muscles: Particularly hip extension and abduction exercises
  • Stabilization exercises that improve pelvic alignment
  • Balance and control exercises in a neutral spine position
  • McKenzie-style extension exercises within a pain-free range
Points to consider
  • Repetitive forward bending, rotation, or lateral flexion movements
  • Stretching or high-intensity strength exercises performed in a painful range
  • Prolonged sitting in a hunched position
  • Sudden movements or heavy lifting
  • Treatment approaches based only on passive methods (rest, traction)
