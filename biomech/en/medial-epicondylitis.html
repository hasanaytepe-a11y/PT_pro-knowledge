PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Golfer's Elbow

Definition and mechanism of development

Golfer's elbow is a tendon injury caused by repetitive and excessive use of the forearm flexor muscles.

During activities involving wrist flexion and pronation, the tendon structures at the medial epicondyle are strained. The pronator teres and flexor carpi radialis are affected most often; the medial collateral ligament and ulnar nerve may also be compressed or irritated.

Exercise approach
  • Activity modification: Avoiding movements that cause pain
  • Forearm flexor and pronator stretching exercises: A pain-free full range of motion is targeted
  • Eccentric strengthening: A controlled, slow lowering phase for the flexor and pronator muscles
  • Shoulder and scapular stabilization exercises: Performed together with core activation
  • Ulnar nerve mobilization: May be applied carefully in cases of nerve irritation
Points to consider
  • Repetitive throwing or throwing movements performed with incorrect technique
  • Exercises involving excessive wrist flexion or pronation
  • Forceful movements that place valgus stress on the elbow (for example, a golf swing)
  • Keeping the elbow bent for prolonged periods or applying pressure to the ulnar nerve
  • Continuing to load despite pain or performing heavily resisted exercises at an early stage
