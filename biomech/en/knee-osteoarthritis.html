PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Knee Osteoarthritis

Definition and mechanism of development

Knee osteoarthritis is a chronic, progressive joint disease involving cartilage breakdown, synovial inflammation, and bone remodeling. Aging, obesity, muscle weakness (particularly the quadriceps), previous injuries, and physical inactivity are the most important risk factors.

The medial tibiofemoral compartment is affected most often. Quadriceps weakness and increased joint loading lead to pain, loss of function, and reduced walking capacity.

Exercise approach
  • Quadriceps strengthening: Seated knee extension, mini squat, wall squat
  • General lower-extremity strengthening: Gluteal, hamstring, and calf muscles
  • Aerobic and strength combination: Walking, stationary cycling, aquatic exercises
  • Balance and low-impact activities: Tai Chi, yoga, gentle balance exercises
  • Frequency: At least 3 days per week, 180 minutes in total (a program of 8 weeks or longer is recommended)
Points to consider
  • High-impact activities (running, jumping, forceful pivoting movements)
  • Prolonged and excessively intense exercise sessions (>90 minutes)
  • Repetitions performed in a painful range or with incorrect technique
  • Inactivity or avoidance of exercise
  • Long-term corticosteroid use and alternative methods with limited evidence
