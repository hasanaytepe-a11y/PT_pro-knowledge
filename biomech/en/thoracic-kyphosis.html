PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Kyphosis

Definition and mechanism of development

Kyphosis is a postural disorder resulting from an increased forward curvature of the spine. It may generally develop due to poor posture, disc degeneration, congenital deformities, or conditions such as Scheuermann's disease.

Weak muscle groups: Thoracic extensors, middle–lower trapezius, rhomboids, and hip extensors
Short/tight muscles: Pectoralis major–minor, upper trapezius, levator scapulae, intercostal muscles, and hip flexors

Exercise approach
  • Pectoral stretching (against a wall, in a doorway, or supine on a bolster)
  • Thoracic self-mobilization (for example, on a foam roller)
  • Thoracic extensor endurance and strengthening exercises
  • Thoracic extension and scapular adduction together with a chin tuck
  • Core stabilization and pelvic neutral-position awareness exercises
Points to consider
  • Excessive intensity of chest exercises, shrug, upright row, back extension, and weighted dips
  • Prolonged forward-bent posture and static sitting at a desk or in front of a screen
  • The habit of bending down to look at a mobile phone
  • Movements that disrupt head–shoulder–pelvis alignment and create excessive thoracic flexion
