PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Tennis Elbow

Definition and mechanism of development

Tennis elbow is a tendon overuse injury characterized by pain around the lateral epicondyle on the outer side of the elbow.

It generally develops as a result of repetitive forearm pronation–supination movements and activities requiring wrist extension. The extensor carpi radialis brevis is affected most often; tendon thickening, microtears, and fibrosis may occur in prolonged cases.

Exercise approach
  • Eccentric wrist extension exercise: 3 sets × 10–15 repetitions daily for 6–12 weeks
  • Isometric and isotonic wrist extensor exercises: Performed within a pain-free range
  • Forearm stretching exercises: Performed regularly to maintain pain-free range of motion
  • An exercise program supported by a wrist brace, ice, or heat application
  • Shoulder and scapular stabilization exercises: Performed to reduce gripping load
Points to consider
  • Repetitive forearm rotation and loading involving full elbow extension
  • Continuing to load during resisted wrist extension or gripping movements
  • Tasks requiring forceful gripping and twisting (for example, assembly, construction, farming)
  • Performing high-resistance exercise or training during a painful period
