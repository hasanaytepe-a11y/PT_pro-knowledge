PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Flat Feet (Pes Planus)

Definition and mechanism of development

Flat feet is a postural disorder characterized by reduction or loss of the medial (inner) arch of the foot. This causes broad contact between the foot and the ground and disrupts load distribution.

Excessive pronation and tibial internal rotation develop as a result of weakness of the intrinsic muscles supporting the plantar arch and laxity of the plantar fascia and spring ligament. In the long term, muscle fatigue and postural imbalance in the lower extremity occur.

Exercise approach
  • Short-foot exercise: Active elevation of the medial arch (6–8 weeks, 3 days per week, 3×10–15 repetitions, 5-second hold)
  • Gluteal strengthening: Clamshell, hip abduction, squat, quadruped hip extension
  • Adductor and calf stretches: Butterfly, lunge, gastrocnemius–soleus stretch
  • Toe-heel and outer-edge walking: Increases tibialis posterior activation
  • TheraBand inversion exercises: Resisted exercises that draw the ankle inward
Points to consider
  • Exercises that place excessive load on the inner side of the foot
  • High-impact activities such as running on hard surfaces, jumping, and climbing stairs
  • Forceful stretching movements involving excessive eversion
  • Use of unsupported footwear or shoes with excessively flexible soles
  • Standing unsupported for prolonged periods or strength training without warming up
