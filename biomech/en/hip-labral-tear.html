PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Hip Labral Tear

Definition and mechanism of development

The hip labrum is a ring of cartilage located along the rim of the acetabulum that retains joint fluid and provides stability to the hip. It may tear as a result of repetitive rotational movements, trauma, or femoroacetabular impingement (FAI).

The anterosuperior labrum is affected most often; strain increases particularly during internal rotation, adduction, and flexion movements.

Exercise approach
  • Pain control: Load reduction, short-term ice application, and relative rest
  • Muscle balance: Strengthening the hip abductor, extensor, and external rotator muscles
  • Core stabilization: Activation of the transversus abdominis and multifidus
  • Balance and movement control: Single-leg stance, coordination, and proprioception exercises
  • Functional progression: Progression from open-chain to closed-chain exercises and retraining of sport patterns
Points to consider
  • Prolonged sitting, jumping, running, or sudden turning movements
  • Excessive hip flexion, extension, abduction, or external rotation
  • Exercises that force movement into a painful range or rapidly increase load
  • Applying the same standard exercises to all patients; individual differences should not be overlooked
