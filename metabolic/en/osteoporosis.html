PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Osteoporosis

Definition and clinical framework

Osteoporosis is a metabolic bone disease in which reduced bone mineral density and deterioration of bone microarchitecture cause bones to become weak, porous, and fragile.

In women, postmenopausal estrogen reduction (Type I) and age-related bone loss (Type II) are particularly important factors. Hip, spinal, and wrist fractures occur most often.

Mechanism of development
  • Aging and hormonal changes (menopause, low testosterone)
  • Physical inactivity and prolonged bed rest
  • Calcium and vitamin D deficiency
  • Smoking, alcohol, and excessive caffeine consumption
  • Long-term use of corticosteroids, antiepileptics, and similar medications
  • Family history and a thin body build
Exercise approach

Goal: To maintain/increase bone mineral density, reduce fracture risk by improving balance and muscle strength, and support functional capacity.

Recommended exercises
  • Weight-bearing aerobic activities: Walking, stair climbing, dancing, light jogging
  • Resistance exercises: With free weights, elastic bands, or machines 2–3 days per week
  • Balance and core exercises: Yoga, Tai Chi, single-leg balance positions
  • Frequency and duration: 3–5 days per week, 30–60 minutes, at moderate intensity
Activities to avoid
  • Sudden, high-impact, or explosive movements (jumping, sharp changes of direction)
  • Exercises that flex or rotate the spine (particularly in advanced osteoporosis)
  • Excessive loading and working within a painful range
  • In severe osteoporosis, lower-impact activities such as cycling or supported walking should be preferred over running.
