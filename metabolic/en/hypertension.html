PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Hypertension

Definition and clinical framework

Hypertension is a chronic cardiovascular disease defined by persistently elevated arterial blood pressure.

Subtypes:

  • Primary (essential) hypertension: The exact cause is unknown; genetic and lifestyle factors play a combined role.
  • Secondary hypertension: Develops due to kidney, hormonal, or vascular diseases, or certain medications.
Mechanism of development
  • Genetic predisposition and age-related arterial stiffness
  • Excessive salt intake, obesity, inactivity
  • Stress, insufficient potassium and magnesium intake
  • Secondary causes: Kidney diseases, hormonal disorders, certain medications
Exercise approach

Aim: To reduce blood pressure by approximately 5–7 mmHg, increase cardiovascular endurance, and support overall cardiovascular health.

Aerobic exercise recommendations
  • Frequency: 5–7 days per week
  • Duration: 30–60 minutes
  • Intensity: Moderate (brisk walking, cycling, swimming, etc.)
Strength (resistance) exercise
  • Frequency: 2–3 days per week
  • Volume: 8–12 repetitions × 1–3 sets
  • Application: It should be performed with regular breathing and without holding the breath (the Valsalva maneuver should be avoided).
Points to consider
  • Resting blood pressure > 180/110 mmHg: Exercise should not be started.
  • Individuals using beta blockers: Because heart rate is suppressed, exercise intensity should be adjusted according to the rating of perceived exertion (RPE) scale (range 11–13, light-to-moderate intensity).
  • Excessive heat, fluid loss, and straining: These conditions should be avoided because they may cause a sudden increase in blood pressure.
