PT Pro – Clinical Information Note

Pacemaker

Definition and mechanism of development

Pacemakers (CRT and ICD) are devices used to regulate rhythm in disorders of the heart's electrical conduction or to stop life-threatening arrhythmias. In patients with an ICD, fear of receiving a shock is common; exercise avoidance, low daily activity, and physiological deconditioning may occur. This naturally reduces exercise capacity.

Regular exercise may reduce the risk of arrhythmia by providing protective effects on autonomic nervous-system balance and cardiac muscle function. However, it should be remembered that a delayed chronotropic response (a delayed increase in heart rate) may occur at the beginning of exercise.

Exercise approach

The exercise program should be planned within the device's technical limits and the framework of clinical safety.

  • Frequency and duration: 20–30 minutes of aerobic exercise, 3–5 days per week.
  • Intensity control: Heart rate should be kept below the ICD's therapy zone; the goal is a “safe range that will not trigger a shock.”
  • Suitable exercises: Walking, cycling, treadmill, arm ergometer, rowing, moderate-intensity resistance training.
  • Monitoring: Use of an HR monitor is recommended; pulse changes may be sudden.
  • Early-stage approach: Upper-extremity loading is reduced during the first weeks after implantation; shoulder movement is restored in a controlled manner.
Points to consider
  • Heart-rate safety limit: During exercise, HR should remain 10–20 bpm below the ICD's shock threshold.
  • Risk of arrhythmia: Sudden accelerations, high-intensity intervals, and Valsalva-type straining may trigger arrhythmia.
  • Early postoperative period: For the first 3–4 weeks, the shoulder should not be raised above 90°; heavy upper-extremity activities should be avoided.
  • Dehydration and heat: Fluid deficiency and excessively hot/cold environments may disrupt rhythm stability.
  • Psychological factors: Fear of shock reduces adherence; calm, reassuring, gradual progression is the best approach.
  • Post-event management: Every shock or rhythm change should be evaluated by the cardiology team.
